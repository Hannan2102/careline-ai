# Safety boundaries

> All data in this project is **synthetic**. This system is a demonstration. It is
> **not HIPAA compliant**, has not been validated clinically, and must not be used with
> real patients or real patient data.

## 1. The three categories

Every inbound utterance is sorted into one of three categories. The category determines
what the system may do, and it is decided by deterministic code — not by the model's
judgement in the moment.

### A. Retrieval of existing medical information — **allowed after verification**

Reading back what a clinician already recorded.

> **Patient:** "What dosage did my doctor prescribe for Metformin?"
> **Agent:** *(verifies identity, reads the active `MedicationRequest`)*
> "Your current prescription on file says Metformin 500 mg, one tablet twice daily with meals."

Rules:
- The dosage string is returned **exactly as stored**. It is passed through as a quoted
  value, not regenerated by the model.
- The response attributes the value to the record ("your prescription on file says").
- If no active prescription is found, the agent says so and offers escalation. It does
  **not** guess.
- The model may not convert, round, restate in different units, or "clarify" the
  instruction.

### B. Administrative workflows — **allowed after verification**

Scheduling, rescheduling, cancelling, refill *requests*, clinic information.

> **Patient:** "I need a refill on my Metformin."
> **Agent:** *(verifies, locates the active prescription, creates a refill request)*
> "I've sent a refill request for your Metformin 500 mg to the clinic for review. You'll
> hear back once a clinician has looked at it."

The system **may**: verify, identify the medication, locate the active prescription,
create a refill request, mark it pending clinician review, confirm it was sent.

The system **must not**: authorise the refill, create a new prescription, change a dose,
substitute or suggest another medication, or imply approval.

### C. New medical or clinical advice — **never**

Diagnosis, treatment, dose changes, side-effect management, symptom triage.

> **Patient:** "My Lisinopril is making me dizzy. Should I take half instead?"
> **Agent:** "I'm not able to advise on changing how you take a medication — that needs a
> clinician. I've recorded what you told me and sent it to our clinical staff to review,
> and someone will get back to you. If you feel faint or unwell right now, please contact
> emergency services."

The agent gives **no** medical answer, records the concern, and escalates.

## 2. Policy table

Each row is a deterministic policy in `backend/app/safety/`, with its own unit test.

| Trigger | Detection | Outcome | Destination | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dose modification requested | dose-change intent + medication entity | refuse, escalate | nurse / clinical | clinical |
| Medication side effect / adverse reaction | symptom attributed to a medication | refuse, escalate | nurse / clinical | clinical |
| Diagnosis requested | "what do I have", symptom → cause | refuse, escalate | clinical | clinical |
| Treatment advice requested | "what should I take/do for" | refuse, escalate | clinical | clinical |
| Potentially urgent symptoms | red-flag term list (chest pain, trouble breathing, stroke signs, severe bleeding, suicidal ideation, anaphylaxis) | **emergency response first**, then escalate | emergency guidance + clinical | urgent |
| Verification failed | N failed attempts | refuse PHI access, escalate | front desk | administrative |
| Patient asks for a human | explicit request | escalate immediately | front desk | patient-requested |
| Low confidence / ambiguous | orchestrator confidence below threshold, or ambiguous entity resolution | stop, escalate | front desk | system-uncertainty |
| Inconsistent or missing EHR data | conflicting or absent records for a verified patient | do not improvise, escalate | front desk / clinical | system-uncertainty |
| Out of scope for the agent | anything with no matching workflow | escalate | front desk | administrative |

**Urgent-symptom handling** is the one case where the agent speaks before it escalates: it
directs the caller to emergency services, states plainly that it cannot help clinically,
and creates an urgent escalation. It never assesses severity, and it never reassures.

## 3. Identity verification

No patient-specific information — appointments, medications, conditions, demographics,
or even confirmation that a person is a patient — is disclosed before verification.

- **Primary factors:** full name + date of birth.
- **Secondary factor** (required when more than one candidate matches, or when the
  primary match is weak): last four digits of the phone on file, or postal code.
- **Exactly one match** → verified. **Zero matches** → nothing disclosed, including whether
  a record exists. **Multiple matches** → request a second factor; never enumerate or hint
  at the candidates.
- Verification is **scoped to the active session** and expires with it. It is stored in
  server-side session state and is never inferred from conversation text, so a caller
  cannot talk their way into a verified state.
- Gated behind verification: medication retrieval, appointment details, refill requests,
  cancellation, rescheduling, and any demographic read.
- Not gated: clinic hours, location, parking, insurance list, new-patient process.

## 4. Escalation and handoff

An escalation preserves context so the human does not restart the conversation.

```
Patient:                John Smith  (synthetic)
DOB verified:           true
Medication:             Lisinopril 10 mg
Concern:                Dizziness after taking medication
Patient question:       "Should I take half?"
AI action:              No dosage recommendation provided
Escalation destination: Nurse / clinical staff
Priority:               Clinical
Session:                sess_01H...
Transcript:             <full turn history attached>
```

Categories: `clinical`, `failed-verification`, `patient-requested`, `system-uncertainty`,
`administrative`. Every escalation is persisted, appears on the dashboard, and carries the
full transcript.

## 5. Non-negotiable invariants

1. The LLM never writes to the EHR. Writes go `tool → service → EHR adapter`.
2. Malformed or unvalidated tool arguments are never executed.
3. Dosage and instruction text is passed through verbatim, never regenerated.
4. Refills are requests, never authorisations.
5. Safety classification runs **before** workflow dispatch and cannot be overridden by
   conversation content, including instructions embedded in what the caller says.
6. Refusals contain no medical content — not even hedged advice.
7. Every safety decision, tool call, and state mutation is audited.
8. The system states plainly that it is an AI assistant when asked.

## 6. The admin dashboard

The dashboard (Phase 10) is a **staff-facing** view, and it sits outside the caller-facing
verification model on purpose: staff see charts without a patient proving identity, which
is exactly how a clinic works and exactly why it needs authentication that this project
does not have.

What that means concretely, stated rather than glossed:

- The dashboard and its read APIs are **unauthenticated**, and are safe here only because
  they are local-only and every record in them is invented.
- Two reads on `EHRProvider` — `list_patients` and `list_appointments` — are not scoped to
  a single patient. Nothing in the agent runtime calls them, and nothing in it may: a
  caller-facing path that can list the roster defeats verification entirely.
- The dashboard is **read-only**. There is no "approve this refill" and no "resolve this
  escalation" button, because a staff action needs a staff identity to record against.
  Authentication is the prerequisite for those buttons, not an enhancement to them.
- The synthetic-data banner is part of the layout on every route and cannot be dismissed,
  so a cropped screenshot still says what it is.

## 7. Path to a regulated deployment

Out of scope here, and listed so the gap is explicit rather than implied: a BAA with every
processor; encryption at rest and in transit with managed keys; authenticated,
role-based staff access; full audit retention and review; minimum-necessary data flows;
vendor zero-retention agreements; caller authentication stronger than name + DOB;
breach procedures; clinical validation and sign-off; and a documented human-in-the-loop
review process. This repository implements the *architecture* that those controls would
attach to — nothing more.
